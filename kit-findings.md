# Kit findings — continuum trial

> **What this is:** Bugs and gaps in the Specifier kit found while running the continuum trial, each with its cause and how it was settled.
>
> **What it is not:** Presentation enhancements (those are in `_archive/root-notes-2026-11-02/GATE-UX-ENHANCEMENTS.md`, all done), or the pre-trial pass (skill-pass.md).

## Finding 1 — check-brd.cjs over-matched a Decision record heading (fixed)

- **Where:** `tools/check-brd.cjs`, the "No journey or NFR section" check.
- **Symptom:** recording a BRD gate decision whose heading quotes question 1 - "What non-functional requirements must the product meet?" - failed the check with "The heading ... belongs in the PRD", blocking the gate.
- **Cause:** the check scanned every heading at level 2 or deeper for journey / NFR / non-functional. A Decision record entry is a level-3 heading that legitimately quotes an NFR question (the decision-record rule requires the heading to be the question exactly as asked).
- **Fix:** scan only level-2 section headings (`h.level === 2`). A real journey or NFR section is a level-2 section and is still caught; level-3 Decision entries are not.
- **Verified:** check-brd passes 10 of 10 on the continuum BRD; the Phase 2 test suite passes 50 of 50.
- **Decided by:** PO, 2026-10-23.

## Finding 2 - umbrella / classification terms reached Gate 2 without being raised at Gate 1

- **Where:** glossary-author, Gate 1 (the Candidates to judge table).
- **Symptom:** in the continuum glossary, abstract umbrella terms (Persona) and a classification that is actually a page column (Persona or access role) passed straight to Gate 2 as if they were settled terms. The specifier caught them at Gate 2 and both were dropped as redundant with the role page's persona / access role column.
- **Cause:** Gate 1's Candidates to judge only flagged borderline-reach candidates (Home, Reading order, Linked item). It did not flag umbrella / aggregate terms (a category that only re-packages terms defined elsewhere) or candidates that are really a column on a kind page rather than a term.
- **To look at (later):** have the harvest step raise umbrella / aggregate candidates, and candidates that map to an existing kind-page column, as Candidates to judge at Gate 1, so redundancy is decided before definitions are written - not caught at Gate 2.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-23 from the continuum trial. Worked around by dropping the two terms at Gate 2.
- **RCA: pending (specifier request).** The specifier felt the glossary carried repeated / overlapping terms and had to point it out themselves. Run a root-cause analysis: why do umbrella or aggregate candidates, and candidates that merely restate a kind-page column, survive the three tests and both gates without the method flagging the repetition? Cover whether Ontology casts too wide, whether the same-meaning test should catch "A is just the set of B and C", and whether a Gate 1 step should actively test each candidate for redundancy against the terms already settled.

## Finding 3 - build-glossary-records.cjs flags the "None." empty-table marker as an unresolved candidate

- **Where:** `tools/build-glossary-records.cjs`, reading each section draft's candidate tables.
- **Symptom:** every empty kind table that a section draft marks with a "None." row (the marker lens1-ontology.md and the section-glossary template sanction for an empty table) is reported as "the candidate 'None.' has no outcome". 12 such findings on the continuum glossary.
- **Cause / ambiguity:** the section-glossary template allows an empty table to be "marked None." but is ambiguous about whether that is a table row or a plain line; the generator treats a "None." row as a real candidate needing a gate outcome.
- **To look at (later):** either the generator should skip a candidate whose word is exactly "None.", or the template should state that an empty table is written as a plain "None." line, not a candidate row. Settle one way and make both match.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-23 from the continuum trial.
- **Status, 2026-11-03:** fixed, not committed. The generator skips a candidate whose word is "None.", the marker the template allows. The template stays as it is. A new test in `glossary.test.cjs` puts a "None." row in a section draft and proves it is not a finding.

## Finding 4 - the register's "Where" link is built from the gate log "Applies to" and can come out broken

- **Where:** `tools/build-glossary-records.cjs`, building the decision register's "Where" column from each gate-log row's "Applies to".
- **Symptom:** register rows showed broken paths such as `deep-dives/analysis/analysis-glossary.md/analysis/analysis-glossary.md-glossary.md`.
- **Cause:** two things together - the gate log's "Applies to" cells linked to the section file (`analysis/analysis-glossary.md`) instead of the section folder (`analysis/`) the gate-log template prescribes, and the generator's link-builder assumed the folder form and compounded the path rather than failing clearly.
- **To look at (later):** have the generator validate the "Applies to" link shape (section folder) and fail with a clear message when it is wrong, rather than emitting a malformed link; and tighten the gate-log template guidance so "Applies to" is always the section folder.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-23 from the continuum trial.
- **Status, 2026-11-03:** fixed, not committed. `build-glossary-records.cjs` checks every "Applies to" first. It must be a link to a section's folder that exists, such as `[claims](claims/)`, or All sections. When one is not, the program writes nothing and names the row and the link to write, such as "Write [analysis](analysis/)". The gate-log template and `output-formats.md` already say "a link to its folder", so they are not changed. 2 new tests in `glossary.test.cjs`.

## Finding 6 - the glossary was built on the raw sources instead of the accepted BRD and PRD

- **Where:** glossary-author execution for continuum - the 12 section drafts, the gate log, and the decision register.
- **Symptom:** the glossary's evidence cites the raw requirement files (mcp-requirements-v4.md, mcp-spec-v4.md, mcp-vision-v1.md) by their own REQ IDs and section numbers, instead of the accepted PRD (continuum-prd.md, by REQ ID) and BRD (continuum-brd.md, by BR ID).
- **Cause (execution error):** the PRD records every requirement verbatim from the source, with the source's own IDs and headings, so the content is identical; the glossary step reached through the PRD to the original file for the citations. This breaks the rule in conventions.md section 4 that only discovery / brd / prd-author read the raw requirement files, and that every later step reads the document just before it in the flow - here, the accepted PRD and BRD (per the glossary-author "Reads" list).
- **Impact:** the glossary does not trace cleanly through the chain (a later change to the PRD would not be reflected via the citations), and terms grounded only in the spec or vision rest on documents that were never the glossary's source. The terms and the hierarchy themselves are correct, because the PRD carries the same requirements; only the sourcing is wrong.
- **To fix:** a re-sourcing pass - re-point the "Source" lines in the section drafts, the gate-log "Applies to"/"Source", and then regenerate the register and section-decisions, so everything cites continuum-prd.md (REQ IDs) and continuum-brd.md (BR IDs). The sections are already the PRD's own headings; the 84 terms stay.
- **Kit angle:** glossary-author has no check program that verifies its evidence points at the accepted BRD and PRD; such a check would have caught this. Consider adding one.
- **RCA: pending (specifier ruling). Root-level mistake - must not recur.** This is a bad build, not a requirement change: the author read the wrong source. The progressive-authority principle is that each document is built from the ones before it, and each step builds on the accepted document just before it (here the PRD and BRD), never the raw sources the PRD and BRD were themselves built from.
  - **The instruction was present and had been read - this was non-application, not a gap.** The rule is stated twice and both were in front of the author: conventions.md section 4 ("Only three skills read your requirement files directly... Every other step reads the document just before it in the flow, and looks up anything else by ID"), read at the start of the run; and glossary-author's "Reads" ("the accepted BRD and PRD"), read when the skill was invoked. The author even quoted section 4 when explaining the error afterward. So the failure was ignoring an explicit, already-read instruction.
  - **Therefore the fix is enforcement, not more prose.** Prose alone did not stop it. Add a check that every glossary citation resolves to the accepted PRD or BRD (fail the gate otherwise), so the rule is mechanically enforced rather than only stated. The RCA should also establish why an in-hand instruction was not applied, since that failure mode is not specific to the glossary.
- **Immediate remediation (this session, Layer 1 done):** the source tags were re-pointed from the raw files to continuum-prd.md (same REQ IDs, same section headings, which the PRD records verbatim) and continuum-brd.md, so the deliverable traces cleanly through the chain and is reviewable by peers. Re-tagged: the 12 section-draft `Source:` headers, the object-terms family table, the plan drift-row requirements label, and the README Sources table (now names the PRD and BRD as the glossary's sources, with the raw files shown as upstream of them). The gate log needed no change. Records regenerated (register, 12 section-decisions, retired-words tables); banned-char sweep clean; glossary re-accepted as version 2.
- **Deliberately left for the RCA (Layer 2, out-of-chain content):** the only raw-file references that remain are the three "slice" citations - gate log G1-10 (history of retiring slice into Story), the frozen Gate-1 assembly draft, and the plan drift row - all pointing at mcp-spec-v4.md. "Slice" is a word that entered only because the raw spec was read; the PRD does not use it. Unpicking it would rework a settled gate decision (the G1-10 story/slice merge), so it is left for the RCA per the specifier's "do not boil the ocean" ruling.
- **Status:** Layer 1 re-tag complete (glossary v2); backlog for the full RCA, the enforcement check (every glossary citation must resolve to the accepted PRD or BRD), and the Layer-2 "slice" removal.
- **Status, 2026-11-03:** the enforcement check is built, not committed. `tools/check-glossary-sources.cjs` reads every file in `deep-dives/`. It fails when a section draft's Source line names neither the PRD nor the BRD, when any file names a requirement file from `sources/`, and when a REQ ID is not in the PRD or a BR ID is not in the BRD. glossary-author runs it before each gate and once more after writing the pages, and holds no gate while it lists something. The README and the master plan's Glossary table name it. 4 new tests in `glossary.test.cjs`. Still open: the full root-cause analysis, and the Layer-2 "slice" removal in continuum.

## Finding 5 - a first-time reader is lost in the glossary files, with no guide on how to consume them

- **Where:** the glossary deliverable, `Output/{project}/glossary/` - the seven pages and the deep-dives folder.
- **Symptom:** someone meeting the glossary for the first time does not know what the files are, which to open first, which are the deliverable versus the evidence, or how to look a term up. The README is the intended gateway, but it reads as one more file among many rather than an obvious front door, and nothing signals "start here".
- **Impact:** the glossary's value depends on people actually using it; if a newcomer cannot find their way in, the terms are not adopted and drift returns.
- **To look at (later):** make the entry point unmistakable and the consumption path explicit - for example a clear "Start here" at the top of README, a one-line "how to look up a term" recipe (find its kind, open that kind page, find it by family), a plain statement that the seven pages are the deliverable and deep-dives is only evidence, and possibly a short orientation note or diagram. Consider whether the kit should state the reading path somewhere a first-timer meets before opening any file.
- **Concrete instance (continuum trial, 2026-10-23):** the specifier looked for "Quick analysis" and "Deep analysis" and could not find them. They are on the Steps page (step-terms.md), because the four-kinds split files a *stage* as a step and its *outputs* as objects. But the Analysis family on object-terms.md (the family-at-a-glance index and the `## Analysis` section) describes the family as "the read's result and findings" and signposts only to Result - it gives no breadcrumb to the quick/deep analysis stages on step-terms.md. The same gap exists for every family whose terms span kinds (Goal, Target, Summary, Plan are each "a step" with object parts on the object page). Only terms-and-relationships.md ties a family's objects and steps together; the kind-pages do not cross-signpost. **Enhancement to consider:** on each kind-page, where a family's terms also live on another kind-page, add a one-line "see also" pointer (for example, in the object-terms Analysis section, "the quick analysis and deep analysis stages are on step-terms.md"). Decided to leave the glossary as-is for now; logged here.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-23 from the continuum trial; cross-signpost instance added 2026-10-23.

## Finding 7 - the glossary retired common English words, so the retired-word scan flags their ordinary use in every later document

- **Where:** `tools/lib/scan-retired-words.cjs`, against the glossary's retired words (`decision` -> Architecture decision, `source` -> Repository); hit while writing the continuum Feature catalog.
- **Symptom:** writing the Feature catalog, ordinary uses of "decision" ("every decision is attributable to a named person" - the wording of BR-12 itself) and "source" ("an optional link to the source", "the step it came from") were flagged as retired words, though the product terms the glossary settled are "Architecture decision" and "Repository". The prose had to be reworded ("decided", "evidence", "origin", "an open point") to pass the check.
- **Cause:** the glossary merged the bare words "decision" and "source" into product terms, but those words also carry everyday English meanings used throughout the requirements. `scan-retired-words.cjs` is context-blind outside quoted text, current-term spans and template lines, so it flags every ordinary occurrence as a retired-word error.
- **Impact:** a broad retirement of a common word leaks into every later document - authors must reword legitimate prose or wrap words in quotes, which makes the documents stilted and the check noisy. It also tempts an author to quote-dodge rather than fix.
- **To look at (later):** at the glossary, retire only the distinguishing product phrase rather than the bare common word (keep "decision" usable; treat only the architecture sense as the term), or scope the retirement; **or** make the scanner context-aware so it flags a retired word only where it plausibly means the product term; **or** let an author mark an occurrence as ordinary-use. Relate to the Finding 2 and Finding 6 glossary RCA.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-23 from the continuum trial; worked around in the catalog by rewording.

## Finding 8 - feature-author gives no rule for how few capabilities (FRDs) to form, so the catalog produced 34

- **Where:** feature-author, the Capability field (the FRD each feature goes in), and `references/feature-catalog.md`.
- **Symptom:** the continuum Feature catalog came out with **34 capabilities** (FRD-1a ... FRD-12d) for 77 features - roughly two to four per PRD section - which would mean 34 FRDs to write and maintain. The specifier flagged it as too many.
- **Cause:** the skill and template define the Capability *format* ("FRD-, the PRD section number and a letter, then a name") and require every feature to name one, but give no rule for how to **size** a capability or how **few** there should be. With a fine feature grain (Finding-adjacent), the author minted a capability per cohesive sub-area, inflating the count.
- **Impact:** the FRD workflow runs once per capability, so the count sets how many FRDs exist; too many fragments the rules across thin documents and multiplies the later work.
- **The 34 -> 16 reshape was a manual patch, not a fix.** In this session the count was corrected by hand at the EPIC grouping gate (relabelling the Capability column via a script) so continuum could proceed. The kit still *produces* 34: a new project runs the same feature-author and hits the same inflation, needing the same hand-correction. The defect is unfixed.
- **The proper fix (kit change, not a per-project patch):**
  1. **feature-author must derive capabilities by rule cohesion so the count is right when the catalog is written** - add an explicit sizing rule (a capability is one FRD's worth of cohesive rules; default roughly one per PRD section; split only where a section holds two genuinely independent rule-sets with different actors), so the author does not mint one capability per feature sub-area in the first place.
  2. **check-features must enforce or flag it** - e.g. fail or warn when the capability count is far out of line with the number of PRD sections or features, so an inflated catalog is caught at the Feature catalog gate rather than relabelled downstream.
  The test of the fix: a fresh run of feature-author on continuum's PRD produces ~16 capabilities with no manual reshape.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-23 from the continuum trial; sharpened 2026-10-26. Root fix still owed; continuum's own catalog carries the hand-patched 16, so the deliverable is fine but the kit is not.
- **Status, 2026-11-03:** fixed, not committed. feature-author has a sizing rule, FR-7 in `references/feature-catalog.md`: a capability is one FRD; start with one for each PRD section; split a section only when it holds two sets of rules that don't depend on each other, and different people act in each. Step 3 and the checklist point to it. `check-features.cjs` lists each PRD section with more than one capability, for the specifier to look at at the gate. It is never a finding, because only a person can judge whether a split is right. This follows the one-per-row check, which also lists and never fails. 1 new test in `features-and-epics.test.cjs`. Not yet proved: a fresh run of feature-author on continuum's PRD giving about 16 capabilities. That waits for the next trial run.

## Finding 9 - check programs read deliverable tables by hard-coded column headers, so a clearer label breaks the check

- **Where:** `tools/check-features.cjs` (and the shared table reader it uses), and the same pattern across the other check programs - a table column is read by its exact header string, for example `p['Piece ID']`, `f['Feature ID']`, `f['Covers (source IDs)']`.
- **Symptom:** the specifier asked to relabel the "Done when" pieces table's **Piece ID** column to **Done-When ID** for clarity. Renaming the header makes check-features fail: it looks the column up by the name "Piece ID", so every row reads as a missing or invalid piece (about 150 findings) and the catalog drops from 9 of 9 to failing.
- **Cause:** the header text doubles as the machine key. The human-facing label and the lookup key are the same string, so they cannot differ - a team cannot rename a column for readability or house style without breaking validation.
- **Impact:** deliverable presentation is locked to the kit's chosen header words. Any relabelling - even a clearer synonym - silently breaks the check, which is a poor failure mode (it reports the content as missing, not the header as renamed).
- **To look at (later):** decouple the display label from the machine key - accept a small set of header aliases per column, match on a normalized or aliased header, or key on column position/role rather than exact text - so "Piece ID" could read "Done-When ID" (and similar relabels elsewhere) without breaking the check. At minimum, have the check detect a renamed/aliased header and say so, rather than reporting every row as missing.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-23 from the continuum trial. Kept the "Piece ID" header so the catalog stays valid (9 of 9).

## Finding 10 - check-frd's Gates "Tied to" name check cannot handle a feature name that contains a comma

- **Where:** `tools/check-frd.cjs`, the "Gates are tied to the features the catalog's Depends on gives" check - the name-extraction regex `/(F-\d+) ([^,(<]+?)(?= \(|,|$)/g`.
- **Symptom:** FRD-8a Navigation ties its Gate In to F-74, whose catalog name is "Work gates, hard conditions, no sign-off". The check captures only "Work gates" (the regex ends the name at the first comma) and compares it to the full catalog name, reporting "F-74 is named 'Work gates', the catalog says 'Work gates, hard conditions, no sign-off'". The FRD is correct - it holds the full name; the finding is false.
- **Cause:** a comma is the delimiter between several tied features in one cell, so the name-capture stops at the first comma. Any feature whose own name contains a comma can therefore never match its catalog name in a Gates "Tied to" cell. There is no way to author the cell so the check passes.
- **Impact:** an otherwise-correct FRD cannot pass check-frd whenever a Gates "Tied to" cell references a comma-named feature. In continuum that is F-74 (hit by Navigation); F-72 "Add, remove, and reorder blocks" is also comma-named and would be hit if ever tied to.
- **To look at (later):** parse the cell by its feature-ID boundaries (split on `<br>` / `• `, then match `F-\d+ (.+)`), or compare by feature ID and look the name up, instead of a comma-terminated regex. Until fixed, Navigation carries one false finding.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-24 from the continuum trial. FRD-8a Navigation is otherwise 38 of 38; this is the only finding and it is a check bug, not a document error.
- **Status, 2026-11-03:** fixed, not committed. `check-frd.cjs` finds each feature in the cell by its ID, looks up its catalog name, and checks that the cell holds that whole name after the ID. A comma in the name no longer cuts it short. A new test in `frd.test.cjs` gives F-01 the name "Send, check and file a claim": the full name passes, and a shortened one is still a finding.

## Finding 11 - for a capability nothing depends on, the Flow ENTRY/EXIT boxes must repeat the whole Gate condition, making an unreadable diagram node

- **Where:** `tools/check-frd.cjs`, "Entry and Exit repeat the Gates conditions word for word"; the FRD template's Flow and Gates.
- **Symptom:** when no outside feature depends on a capability, its Gate Out condition is all the covered features' "Done when" lines joined (one per line with `<br>• `). The check requires the flow's EXIT box - a Mermaid node label - to repeat that string verbatim. For FRD-11a Case-driven output and FRD-10a Evidence the EXIT node becomes a four- or five-sentence block with bullets and `<br>`, which renders as an unreadable diagram box. (Two forks wrote a short placeholder in the box instead and failed the check.)
- **Impact:** the diagram, meant to be the readable view of the flow, carries a giant node; authors are tempted to paraphrase the box and then fail the check. The same applies to the ENTRY box when the capability has several Depends-on features.
- **Concrete proof (specifier-reported, 2026-10-26).** A Setup-style FRD's ENTRY box came out as **four stacked bullets, each a composite sentence of three to four behaviours** (type a provider + model + key and save without checking; connect a host + register the app + disconnect to forget; add or remove repositories + unreachable-until-access-returns + same experience throughout; run a test + one request + reply-or-message + see the last result and time). The flow **diagram** then shows only about six nodes - ENTRY, one state node, a decision, two outcomes, EXIT - with **all the real steps buried inside the ENTRY and EXIT endpoint boxes**. So the diagram, whose job is to *show* the flow, hides the steps in its endpoints and shows almost nothing in between; and each buried bullet is itself a bundle of behaviours (Finding 13), so an essential step can be folded away unseen. This is the realisation of the specifier's fear that "essential steps may be missed".
- **It is happening in continuum, not only in illustrations.** Any capability that depends on several features inherits each one's composite "Done when" as an ENTRY bullet: FRD-5a Target (three dependencies), FRD-6a Summary (two), FRD-3a Setup (one, composite). The ENTRY/EXIT walls are in the accepted FRDs.
- **To look at (later):** let the ENTRY/EXIT boxes carry a **short** label (e.g. the gate's name or a one-line summary) while the verbatim condition lives only in the Entry/Exit text lines and the Gates table; **and have the diagram render the capability's actual steps as its nodes**, not collapse them into two giant endpoints. Verify the verbatim condition on the text lines, not the diagram node. Combine with Finding 13 so each step-node is one behaviour.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-24; sharpened 2026-10-26 with the specifier's proof. This is the highest-visibility presentation-and-traceability defect in the FRD output: the diagram is currently close to useless for reading a capability's steps, and the fix must make the diagram show the steps, not the gate walls.

## Finding 12 - the "**Title** — text" requirement shape is required downstream by check-frd but is neither a stated rule nor enforced at the PRD

- **Where:** the PRD template (`_shared/templates/prd.md`), section "Requirements, word for word"; `tools/check-prd.cjs`; `tools/check-frd.cjs`.
- **The mismatch (as the specifier found it):**
  - **PRD template:** the `**Title** — text` shape appears only in the example cell (`**Finance approval** — Claims over 500 need finance approval.`). There is no written rule that every requirement must use it; the instruction is soft ("its title in bold when the file gives one").
  - **check-prd:** does not check for a bold title or the "—" separator at all.
  - **check-frd:** hard-requires it - it reads each PRD requirement with `/^\*\*(.+?)\*\* — (.+)$/`, and any requirement not in that shape is simply not picked up, so check-frd then reports "REQ-x.y is not in the PRD" and fails every FRD that covers it.
- **Impact:** a PRD can be written and **accepted** (check-prd passes) with one or more requirements not in `**Title** — text` shape; the failure only surfaces two stages later, when an FRD that covers such a requirement is checked, and the only fix is to change the already-accepted PRD through a change. The shape is effectively mandatory but is enforced far too late. continuum avoided this only because its PRD happened to put every requirement in the shape.
- **To look at (later):** make the rule explicit and enforce it where the requirement is written - state in the PRD template and conventions that each "Requirement as written" is `**Title** — text` (bold title, an em-dash, then the text), and have check-prd enforce it so a non-conforming requirement is caught at the PRD gate. Alternatively, relax check-frd to tolerate a requirement that has no bold title. Either way, the stated rule, the upstream check and the downstream check should agree.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-26 from the continuum trial (specifier-reported).
- **Status, 2026-11-03:** fixed, not committed. The rule was already written in the PRD template and in prd-author, step 4: a short title in bold, then a dash, then the requirement. `check-prd.cjs` now fails a Requirement row whose simple words are in any other shape, so it is caught at the PRD's gate. `check-frd.cjs` is not changed. The PRD checklist names the check. 2 new tests in `requirements.test.cjs`: no bold title, and a hyphen in place of " — ".

## Finding 13 - rules and flow steps inherit bundled behaviours from composite "Done when" pieces, and "one behaviour per rule" is not enforced

- **Where:** feature-author (cutting a "Done when" into pieces), frd-author (rules and flow), and `tools/check-frd.cjs` (which lists "each rule states one behaviour" as **not** program-checked).
- **Symptom:** a single "Done when" piece can bundle several actions in one clause, and the FRD rule that mirrors it then states several behaviours in one rule, shown as one flow step. In continuum, F-19's piece is "The quick analysis can be paused, resumed, stopped and restarted while it runs" - four control actions - and it becomes one rule (F-19.1) and one step. The convention says "**One behaviour per rule. No bundled and/or - split into two rules**", but check-frd does not count behaviours inside a rule, so a bundled rule passes. The ENTRY/EXIT boxes compound the effect by echoing the whole composite "Done when" as one sentence (see Finding 11).
- **Impact:** this is the "essential steps may be missed" risk the specifier raised. A coarse rule becomes **one** acceptance criterion downstream where several are due - e.g. "stopped" and "restarted" get one AC instead of each being tested - so a real behaviour can be under-specified without any check catching it. The flow *looks* complete (every rule appears, one per step) while a step quietly folds three or four actions.
- **To look at (later):**
  1. **At feature-author:** when a "Done when" names several actions, cut the piece to one action each (so the pieces, not just the whole line, are atomic), or require the piece to be proved by one rule per action.
  2. **At frd-author / check-frd:** enforce one behaviour per rule - flag a rule whose text is an un-split list of verbs joined by "and"/"or"/commas - so a bundled rule is caught, not waved through as "not program-checked".
  3. **For the gate echo** (the long ENTRY/EXIT sentences): see Finding 11.
- **Note on what is already sound:** the flow *mechanics* are enforced and reliable - exactly one rule per step, every rule appears in the flow, every "Done when" piece is proved by a rule - so nothing that is a rule is dropped. The gap is purely the *granularity* of the rules themselves, inherited from composite pieces and not checked.
- **Status:** backlog, raised 2026-10-26 from the continuum trial (specifier-reported). The 16 accepted FRDs carry some bundled rules (e.g. F-19.1); a later pass can split them, and acceptance criteria should treat such a rule as several conditions until then.
- **Status, 2026-11-02:** fixed in part, not committed. `check-frd.cjs` now lists each rule that may hold two behaviours, through `tools/lib/one-per-row.cjs`, for a person to judge.
