1The workflows at a glance

Four workflows run one after another. The Glossary package sits on top of them all, because every workflow uses the Glossary. The requirement-update workflow runs whenever you raise a change.

Rules that apply at every step
  • Before any step writes, scaffold-project.cjs makes sure the project's folders exist.
  • Every skill that writes a document runs two things inside itself, after drafting and before its check:
    • probing-questions-author, which turns each gap into a question, rated for impact and severity
    • grade-questions.cjs, which places each question in its group, sets the order, and checks that every gap names its question
    The questions go to the one Question log, and the workflows gain no extra steps.
  • Before every gate, the document's own check program runs. It prints what you read first at the gate, and its last block goes into the document's Check record. From the Feature catalog on, it also looks for retired glossary words.
  • At every gate, the questions come first, in the order grade-questions.cjs sets:
    • the Must answer questions, one at a time
    • the Should answer questions, one at a time
    • the Can move ahead questions, as one list with their recommended answers
    • then the gate's own question
  • An open question never fails a check, and never stops an acceptance, except under two guards:
    • a Must answer question is answered before any FRD that builds on it is accepted
    • a Must answer question raised while writing stories is answered before those stories are marked ready
  • At every gate you see where the draft goes beyond the client's words. After you accept, you get the list for the client, in two halves:
    • what your decisions changed or added, to confirm with the client
    • what we still need from the client
  • Every time you accept a document, accept.cjs:
    • saves a copy of the document in the Version log
    • saves a copy of the Question log as it stands
    • rebuilds the Traceability map
    • updates any open change
  • After every acceptance, status.cjs lists every Must answer question still open, and names the next step.
  • Every living document has its own decision record, written at its gate.
  • After the third "no" at the same gate, the skill offers:
    • park it, only at an FRD's gate or an EPIC's gates
    • accept it as it is
    • one more round
  • Sources are read only in the Requirements workflow, and in a change by change-impact-author and the version comparison, for the files you bring. Later steps read their nearest input and look up anything else by ID.
  • When a step finds a mistake in a document you've already approved, it pauses and you raise it as a change. It goes through the requirement-update workflow like any other change.
  • A small update, such as a late answer to a Low impact question, is inserted where it belongs, and nothing is re-run.
  • On a re-run of the whole workflow, each question you answered before is asked again, with your earlier answer recommended and dated.
  • NFRs live only in the PRD, each with an ID. EPIC briefs only list which NFR IDs apply.
  • Each requirement in the PRD records where its wording came from: the document and its version.
  • Only the specifiers, the BA and the PO, make decisions. Skills read, draft and record; programs check, route and generate.

Workflow 3, once per capability, all up frontFRD

The FRD's gate
  • The Must answer questions this FRD builds on come first, one at a time. While one is open, the FRD can't be accepted: you answer it, or park the FRD.
  • A parked FRD goes to the back of the queue and is written once the others are done.
  • route-next-capability.cjs puts a capability waiting on a Must answer question last, after the others and before parked ones, so you work first on what you can finish. It comes back once its question is answered.

Workflow 4, once per EPICStories

The order, the checks and the ready guard
  • The next EPIC is taken in EPIC register order. A parked EPIC goes to the back of the queue, and any EPIC that depends on it waits too, so nothing is built on top of it. When only those are left, the parked EPIC comes back and is redone.
  • Before each gate, its check runs:
    • check-epic-brief.cjs, for the EPIC brief
    • check-stories.cjs, for the split, the grooming and the criteria
    • coverage-check.cjs too, before the last gate
  • While a Must answer question raised in these stories is open, they can't be marked ready.
  • route-next-epic.cjs puts an EPIC waiting on a Must answer question last, as it does a parked one. It comes back once the question is answered.

Whenever you raise a changeRequirement-update

How to read this flow
  • When glossary-check finds a word that doesn't match the Glossary, glossary-disposition settles each one first, at its own gate, so the impact analysis works with agreed words. For each word you choose:
    • fix the document
    • change the Glossary
    • add a new word
    Then build-glossary-records.cjs and build-retired-words-tables.cjs rebuild what is filled from the gate log.
  • From the challenge to your gate, every step runs inside change-impact-author, which holds the gate:
    • adversarial-check
    • find-linked.cjs
    • feature-impact, once for each linked item
    • size-rules.cjs
    • recommend.cjs
    • check-change-report.cjs
  • You raise the change. It can come:
    • from the client
    • from you
    • from a step that found a mistake in an approved document
  • If you raise several changes at once, change-impact-author splits them, and each one runs this flow on its own.
  • If the verdict is blocked, the report pauses after the challenge. The question that blocks it goes to the Question log and is asked first, in the same shape as every other question. The report carries on once you answer.
  • If the challenge or the report check fails, the report goes back to change-impact-author. Their findings are kept in the report.
  • The size is tentative until you accept it at your gate.
  • The Extend and Update paths start with glossary-disposition when the change renames an agreed word. That adds one gate.
  • The Extend and Update paths also update the BRD when it holds the changed thing. You see the proposed edit before you choose.
  • An answer that arrives later comes in as a change, a second way in. It starts from what the question says it changes.
  • A small update is inserted, not re-run. When an answer only fills the places that name its question, or a change only rewords a requirement, you can choose "insert it" at the recommendation's gate: insert-answer.cjs or copy-wording.cjs writes it in place, and nothing else runs.
  • A dramatic change is flagged. When a change reaches more than half of the business requirements, the recommendation says so, and offers a re-run of the whole workflow as one of your choices.

A change that arrives while work is in progress
  • The change is sized against whatever is in progress:
    • an EPIC
    • an FRD
    • the Feature catalog
    • the EPIC grouping
  • Small: no feature joins or leaves the piece in progress, and an EPIC's story split still holds, apart from one added story at most. The change is folded in: the work carries on from its gate, on the new versions.
  • Large: any of these:
    • features join or leave the piece in progress
    • an EPIC's story split no longer holds
    • several pieces reshape
    The piece is parked and goes to the back of the queue. Anything that depends on it waits. The change closes once its own steps are done.
  • Nothing in progress: nothing is sized. The change just runs, and a story already with the team gets a new story for the difference.
  • A draft you haven't approved yet: not a change. You give the feedback at that document's own gate.
  • Every document that exists counts, drafted or accepted: a change updates a drafted one in its next draft, and gives an accepted one a new version.
  • The report proposes the size, and you decide it at the recommendation gate.
